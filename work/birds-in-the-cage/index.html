Birds inthe Cage

The frame is the cage.

Birds have long been romanticized as symbols of freedom. Yet freedom itself is often defined by those who observe it.

We recognize confinement through visible boundaries—cages, walls, and barriers. But what about the boundaries we cannot see?

Birds in the Cage explores the paradox of freedom and confinement, questioning whether freedom exists independently of the way we perceive it.

01 / The Visible Cage

Confinement is easy to recognize when it has walls.

A cage creates a visible distinction between freedom and captivity.

Its boundaries are physical, recognizable, and seemingly absolute.

Yet these structures do more than restrict movement. They shape how we understand the space within them—and how we imagine the world beyond.

02 / The Invisible Cage

Not all cages have bars.

Freedom is often imagined as the absence of physical boundaries.

Yet invisible structures continue to shape the possibilities of movement, choice, and existence.

What appears to be an open space may still operate within limits we have learned not to question.

03 / The Frame Is the Cage

To photograph is to define a boundary.

Photography introduces another form of confinement.

Through the act of framing, the photographer determines what remains visible and what disappears.

A living subject is extracted from its surroundings, suspended in time, and reconstructed within the limits of an image.

The frame does not simply contain what we see. It shapes what we are able to understand.
